SYSTEM OVERVIEW: FEE-BASED VS FUND-BASED

Fee-Based Services (Advisory / Non-Fund)

Financial institution acts as consultant/agent without deploying its own balance sheet funds directly.

Examples: Merchant Banking, Credit Rating, Depository System

Fund-Based Services (Asset-Based)

Institution provides direct capital/funds or commits balance sheet assets to earn interest/spread.

Examples: Factoring, Forfaiting, Leasing, Venture Capital, Securitisation

FACTORING EFFECTIVE COST CALCULATION

Problem Statement:

A firm sells receivables of ₹10,000,000 to a factor. Factor advances 80% immediately at 12% p.a. interest, charges 2% factor commission on total invoice. Collection period = 90 days (0.25 years).

1. Advance Amount: 80% of ₹10,000,000 = ₹8,000,000

2. Interest Cost: ₹8,000,000 × 12% × 0.25 = ₹240,000

3. Commission: 2% of ₹10,000,000 = ₹200,000

4. Total Cost: ₹240,000 + ₹200,000 = ₹440,000

5. Net Cash Received: ₹8,000,000 - ₹440,000 = ₹7,560,000

Effective Annualized Cost Rate:

(Total Cost / Net Cash) × (365 / Days) = (440,000 / 7,560,000) × 4 = 23.28% p.a.

FORFAITING DISCOUNT NET PROCEEDS

Problem Statement:

An Indian exporter holds export Promissory Notes of $500,000 due in 3 years. Forfaiter charges 8% p.a. discount rate, 0.5% commitment fee p.a., plus grace days.

• Discount Rate (8%): Deducted upfront on note face value per tenor.

• Commitment Fee (0.5%): Paid for reserving funds until drawdown.

• Grace Days: Compensation for banking collection delays.

• Bank Aval: Guarantee provided by importer's bank prior to forfaiting.

Exam Note:

Forfaiting is 100% Without Recourse. Exporter surrenders notes and derecognises asset completely!
